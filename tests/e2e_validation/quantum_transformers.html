A Survey of Quantum Transformers

Architectures, Challenges and Outlooks — Zhang et al., Nov 2025

The Central Question

Why Quantum Transformers? The Motivation

  • Classical Transformer self-attention: complexity grows as O(N2) with sequence length N
  • Quantum Machine Learning (QML) leverages superposition, entanglement, and interference
  • Core goal: reduce quadratic scaling while maintaining or improving expressiveness
  • Since 2022: rapid expansion of research into Quantum Transformer architectures
  • Applications span NLP, computer vision, high-energy physics, chemistry, finance, fluid dynamics

Classical Transformer: Quick Recap

  • Self-Attention: each token attends to all others; captures long-range dependencies
  • QKV Mapping: input projected to Queries (Q), Keys (K), Values (V) via learned matrices
  • Attention matrix: 𝐀=softmax(QKTdk); output: 𝐘=𝐀𝐕
  • Multi-head attention: h independent attention heads concatenated and projected
  • Feed-Forward Network: FFN(𝐙)=max(0,ZW1+𝐛1)𝐖2+𝐛2
  • Residual + LayerNorm: 𝐙=LayerNorm(𝐗in+f(𝐗in))

Quantum States and Data Encoding

  • A quantum state of n qubits: |ψ⟩=∑i=02n−1αi|i⟩; amplitudes satisfy ∑i|αi|2=1
  • Qubit Encoding (QE): classical scalar x encoded via rotation gate Ry(x/2)|0⟩
  • Amplitude Encoding (AE): vector 𝐱=(x0,…,x2n−1) embedded directly in quantum amplitudes — exponentially space-efficient
  • Data Encoding is critical: determines how classical information enters quantum circuits
  • Trade-off: expressive encodings (AE) require complex circuit constructions

Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs)

  • PQCs extend quantum circuits with trainable parameters θ
  • Unitary: U(θ)=UL(θL)⋯U2(θ2)U1(θ1)
  • Used in Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs): optimize C(θ)=⟨ψ(θ)|H|ψ(θ)⟩
  • Classical optimizer updates θ; combines quantum expressiveness with classical flexibility

Key Quantum Tools: Kernel, Swap Test, QSVT

  • Quantum Kernel: maps classical data to Hilbert space; K(xi,xj)=|⟨ψ(xi)|ψ(xj)⟩|2; enables quantum-enhanced similarity computation
  • Swap Test: quantum circuit that estimates 1+|⟨ψ|ϕ⟩|22; measures similarity between two quantum states without full state tomography
  • Hadamard Test: estimates Re(⟨ψ|U|ψ⟩) using ancilla qubit; evaluates expectation values of unitary operators
  • QSVT (Quantum Singular Value Transformation): powerful framework for quantum linear algebra; enables exponential speedups for matrix operations in fault-tolerant settings

QKV-Only Quantum Mapping

  B --> C[PQC maps to Q K V]
  C --> D["Classical: softmax(QK^T/sqrt(dk))"]
  D --> E[Output Y = AV]
  style C fill:#58a6ff,stroke:#58a6ff,color:#0d1117
  style D fill:#f0883e,stroke:#f0883e,color:#0d1117
  • Only the Q/K/V mapping step is quantized
  • Attention score computation remains classical
  • Representative: QSANN (Li et al., 2022) — first quantum self-attention neural network
  • Uses PQC to generate Q, K, V in quantum Hilbert space; measurements yield classical vectors
  • Advantage: compatible with standard Transformer architectures
  • Limitation: does not reduce the O(N2) classical attention bottleneck

Quantum Pairwise Attention

  • Q, K, V remain as quantum states throughout attention computation
  • Pairwise token similarities computed using quantum tools:
  • Swap Test: directly estimates |⟨qi|kj⟩|2 — inner-product similarity
  • Hadamard Test: extracts real part of inner products
  • Quantum Kernel: computes generalized similarity f(qi,kj)
  • Deeper quantization than QKV-only — attention itself runs on quantum hardware
  • Quantum Mixed-State Self-Attention Network (QMSAN): uses swap test for pairwise similarities
  • Validated on small NLP classification tasks; shows competitive accuracy

  • Captures global token dependencies in a single quantum transformation
  • No explicit pairwise similarity computation (avoids O(N2) classically)
  • Uses holistic quantum transformations: 𝒰mix({xi}i=1N)
  • Potentially achieves sub-quadratic speedup
  • Still an open research question whether genuine algorithmic advantage is achieved
  • Examples: Kerenidis et al. (2022), Alessandro et al. (2024)

Real Quantum Hardware: Wukong Experiment

  • He et al. (2024) executed a quantum Transformer on the 72-qubit Wukong superconducting quantum computer (Chinese Academy of Sciences)
  • Architecture similar to QSANN with improved PQC structure for single-qubit measurement
  • Parallel strategies at attention and batch levels; theoretical training speedup of 3×bs×n
  • Results on MC and RP datasets: MC 100% accuracy, RP 83.87% — slightly outperformed the simulator
  • First demonstration of quantum self-attention feasibility on real NISQ hardware
  • Demonstrates noise-adaptive design can overcome NISQ imperfections

Key Challenges and Open Questions

Section divider

Technical Challenges in Quantum Transformers

Training Challenges

  • Barren Plateaus: gradients vanish exponentially with system size — training becomes intractable for large PQCs
  • Generalization: quantum models may overfit on small datasets
  • Data encoding overhead: classical-to-quantum encoding costs can negate quantum speedups

Hardware Challenges

  • NISQ constraints: high error rates, limited qubit counts, and short coherence times
  • Scalability: unclear if advantages persist beyond small benchmarks
  • Measurement bottleneck: extracting classical information requires many measurement shots

PQC vs. QLA: Where Are We?

PQC-based (Near-term)

  • Demonstrated on real NISQ hardware
  • Quantum Enhanced Representation validated on small benchmarks
  • Open question: genuine sub-quadratic speedup?

QLA-based (Long-term)

  • Theoretical exponential speedup promised
  • Requires fault-tolerant quantum computers
  • Training feasibility and barren plateaus remain fundamental obstacles

Key Takeaways: Quantum Transformers Survey

  • Core motivation: classical Transformer self-attention is O(N2); quantum approaches aim to reduce this bottleneck
  • Two paradigms: PQC-based (near-term, NISQ) and QLA-based (long-term, fault-tolerant)
  • Four PQC strategies: QKV-only mapping, quantum pairwise attention, quantum holistic attention, quantum-assisted optimization — ordered by depth of quantization
  • Demonstrated so far: quantum enhanced representation on small benchmarks; first real hardware execution on Wukong (72 qubits)
  • Open questions: genuine algorithmic speedup, scalability, barren plateaus, and training feasibility remain unresolved
  • Outlook: promising for physics/chemistry domains with inherently quantum data; hybrid designs most practical near-term
